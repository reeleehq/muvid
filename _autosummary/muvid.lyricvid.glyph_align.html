muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align¶

Per-glyph timing: when was each written character sung? (muvid#142)

A word-level alignment says when バカンス was sung; a learner watching katakana needs to see バ, カ, ン and ス light up one by one, each at the moment its sound starts. This module refines any word-timed TimedText (from Suno’s own timestamps, an enhanced LRC, a whisper match…) into one with measured glyphs.

How: each lyric line is cut out of the separated vocal stem, widened by margin_s on both sides, and force-aligned with a CTC model (torchaudio’s MMS_FA, trained on romanised speech in 1,000+ languages) against the line’s romanisation, one romaji chunk per glyph, with a <star> token either side to absorb breaths and ad-libs. Aligning per line, inside the coarse word window, is what keeps one mis-sung line from dragging every later one.

Romanisation is a strategy slot (romanize=): romanize_kana() covers katakana and hiragana, including ー (lengthens the previous vowel), ッ (doubles the next consonant) and the small ャュョァィゥェォ combinations. Another script needs another romaniser, nothing else.

Verification lives here too, because a timing nobody checked is a guess: onset_report() measures how close glyph starts sit to acoustic onsets in the vocal stem, against a random-jitter baseline, and word_agreement() lists the words where two timings disagree. reconcile() then acts on what a list of Detector s distrusts — each names words and a remedy (replace: fall back to the vendor window; drop: it was never sung). unsung() drops a line the aligner could not place over which the vocal stem is silent (muvid#144); silent_glyph() flags a word with one silent sounded glyph inside a sung line (muvid#156).

Licensing — read before shipping anything: the MMS_FA weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0 and htdemucs (vocal separation) is CC-BY-NC too. So this is an opt-in extra (muvid[lyricvid-glyphs]), never a default, never on the prod connector, and it never downloads weights unless the caller says allow_download=True (or MUVID_ALLOW_WEIGHT_DOWNLOAD=1).

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.apply_timing_overrides(reconciled, fine, records, *, overrides)[source]¶

Put recorded human judgements back over reconcile()’s result.

overrides is per-song DATA, {"<line index>": "fine"} — the line’s 0-based position in the transcript fine was refined from (string keys, as JSON writes them, or ints). It belongs in the song’s own spec beside the lyrics, never in code: it says “on THIS song, this line’s vendor window is junk and the aligner’s times sit on onsets” (muvid#150, used on five songs). Each value is one of TIMING_OVERRIDES.

reconciled and records are what reconcile(fine, coarse, ...) returned; words are matched by position, so a line is restored exactly — even one unsung() dropped, since a person said it was sung. Only the named line changes: a neighbour disordered() flagged together with it keeps its vendor window unless it is named too. reconcile’s records still say what reconcile did; these say what undid it. Returns the timing and one record per word whose timing changed (same keys as reconcile()’s; why is "override"). Empty or None overrides return reconciled unchanged.

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.lost(vocals, *, disagree_s=0.3, min_glyph_s=0.04, tol_s=0.08)[source]¶

Words the aligner could not place, as unsung()’s among.

A word is lost when it is squeezed(), or when it disagrees(), its fine start is off every onset, AND that start lies inside ANOTHER line’s vendor window (bias-shifted): the aligner, finding nothing to put it on, pushed it onto a neighbour. That is the unsung intro repeat of muvid#150, whose first word disagreed instead of squeezing, so a squeeze-only among never saw the whole line as flagged.

Each condition guards against deleting sung lyrics, because the remedy this feeds is drop:

  • A disagreeing word the aligner put ON an onset was sung somewhere.

  • A word left in its OWN line’s neighbourhood can be one the vendor merely placed late, by more than the aligner’s search margin (MARGIN_S). Its window is then quiet, and without the third condition the line is deleted (review of muvid#154).

Remedy: replace (it only names words; unsung() decides).

Return type:

Detector

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.misplaced(vocals, *, disagree_s=0.3, tol_s=0.08)[source]¶

Disagreeing words where the onsets side with the vendor. Remedy: replace.

disagrees() falls back to the vendor time whenever the two timings differ, but on a song where the fine aligner is good the vendor is usually the one that is wrong (muvid#150: seven words flagged, and every fine time sat within ~60 ms of a stem onset while the vendor times were up to 10 s off, one of them before the end of the previous line). So a word is flagged only when its fine start is off every onset (tol_s) AND its bias-shifted vendor start is on one: the evidence has to point at the vendor, not just away from the aligner. Use it in place of disagrees() when you have the stem (see stem_detectors()).

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.reconcile(fine, coarse, *, disagree_s=None, min_glyph_s=None, detectors=None)[source]¶

Distrust the fine timing where a detector says so; remedy it; say where.

detectors defaults to default_detectors() (squeezed and disagrees, both replace; disagree_s and min_glyph_s tune them, and are refused alongside explicit detectors, which they could not reach). Add unsung() when you have the vocal stem:

reconcile(fine, coarse, detectors=(*default_detectors(), unsung(stem)))

replace puts a word back in its COARSE window, shifted by the median offset between the two timings over the words no detector flagged (vendor times run late; the bias is measured, not assumed), glyphs spread evenly and marked measured=False. drop removes it, and any line or section left empty. Returns the timing and one record per affected word: why (the deciding detector), detail, remedy, flagged_by (every detector that flagged it) and used (the start it got; None when dropped). mute (see silent_glyph()) keeps the word and its times and sets sung=False on the glyphs the detector names (the record lists their indices as glyphs); it ranks below replace, so a word another detector distrusts is re-timed and its silence verdict, measured on the distrusted time, is not kept. A ー after a muted glyph keeps sung=True (it is never judged); and a fine timing override restores the line whole, sung included.

Return type:

tuple[TimedText, list[dict[str, Any]]]

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.repair_spills(tt, onsets, *, gap_s=0.05, min_step_s=0.08, clear_s=0.1, min_tail_s=0.05)[source]¶

Pull a line’s glyphs back off the NEXT line, where they aligned (muvid#155).

Seen on a real song: ハット’s ト aligned onto the next line’s ト. It also follows from reconcile() itself: a line sent back to its vendor window can end on a neighbour the aligner had right. A glyph (not a line’s first) starting within gap_s of the next line’s first glyph, or after it, has SPILLED. It moves to the first of onsets (the vocal stem’s, see stem_onsets()) at least min_step_s after the glyph before it and clear_s before the next line — or, with none there, midway between that floor and gap_s before the next line, so it is never left spilled — and is marked measured=False. Every glyph after the first spilled one moves with it: they share that room in equal slices, each taking the first onset in its own slice, so no glyph is left on the next line for want of room an earlier one took.

Then, in that line only, nothing stays lit into the next line or over a moved glyph: the glyph before a moved one ends no later than it starts, a moved glyph ends by the next line, and each word spans its glyphs (from its first glyph’s start, to its last glyph’s end or min_tail_s past its start — CTC spans are spiky) and ends by the next line too. A glyph with no room before the next line (the line itself already sits on it) is left alone: moving it would put it before the glyph it follows, and a whole line on its neighbour is disordered()’s case, not a spill.

Run it on reconcile()’s result (and after apply_timing_overrides()). Returns the timing — the very object passed in when nothing spilled — and one record per moved glyph: text, line, why ("spilled"), detail, remedy ("onset" or "midpoint"), fine (its start before) and used (its start now).

Return type:

tuple[TimedText, list[dict[str, Any]]]

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.romanize_hepburn(text)[source]¶

Display romanisation (modified Hepburn) of kana text: what a learner reads.

romanize_kana() is an ALIGNMENT romaniser — ー is silent there, because it has no onset. Read on screen it needs a macron. Also ン before a vowel or y takes an apostrophe, ッ before ch is t, and glides keep their w/y (muvid#145).

>>> romanize_hepburn("バス バス スタート")
'basu basu sutāto'
>>> romanize_hepburn("ハンバーガー")
'hanbāgā'
>>> romanize_hepburn("カップ マッチ シャツ")
'kappu matchi shatsu'
>>> romanize_hepburn("キンエン ウィンドウ")
"kin'en windou"

Anything that is not kana is KEPT, not dropped — kanji, digits, Latin, an unknown kana — because a caption that silently loses a word looks right:

>>> romanize_hepburn("東京 バス、スタート! Café")
'東京 basu, sutāto! Café'

Input is NFKC-normalised first, so half-width (ﾊﾞｽ) and decomposed (ハ + combining ゙) kana read as the composed glyphs. Hiragana long vowels spelt with a second vowel (とうきょう) keep both letters: telling ou the long vowel from o``+``u needs a dictionary.

Return type:

str

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.silent_glyph(vocals, *, peak_db=-25.0, median_db=-28.0, window_s=(0.06, 0.4), frame_s=0.01, remedy='drop')[source]¶

Words with a sounded glyph whose own window is silent in the stem (muvid#156).

squeezed() sees glyph starts too close together and unsung() sees whole quiet lines; neither sees ONE silent mora inside a sung line. That happens when the transcript lists ハッカー and the singer sang ハッ (CTC must still place カ, and puts it in the ッ gap: measured=True, 61 ms, 30-40 dB under the singing), and when an unmeasured glyph keeps a vendor fallback time that lands in a gap. A consumer that plays per-glyph clips plays silence or a click for these.

A glyph’s window runs from its start to the next sounded glyph’s start (ー and ッ make no sound of their own and are neither judged nor ends), clamped to window_s; its levels are dB under the stem’s loud level. It is silent when its peak is under peak_db AND its median under median_db. Measured on 21 songs this flagged 13 words in 8, each checked against the stem envelope; sung-but-devoiced glyphs (デス ス) stay above. A window running past the stem’s end, or a digitally silent stem, is not judged (the safe direction for a remedy that deletes words).

Remedy: drop at WORD level by default (a per-glyph consumer, such as one playing a clip per kana, wants the word out of its timing); mute marks only the silent glyphs sung=False and keeps the rest of the word and its times (a lyric video keeps ハッ of ハッカー); replace falls back to the vendor window. drop deletes sung text in a lyric video and outranks replace, so it also turns a recoverable squeezed word into a deletion: opt-in, NOT part of stem_detectors(). Only wide (kana, CJK) glyphs are judged; words without glyphs are not judged and do not end a neighbour’s window.

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.unsung(vocals, *, among=None, quiet_db=-40.0, quiet_share=0.5, min_quiet_s=1.0, frame_s=0.05)[source]¶

Lines a vendor transcript lists but nobody sang. Remedy: drop.

A LINE is unsung when both hold: among (default squeezed()) distrusts every word in it — the aligner, searching the line’s window, found nothing to put them on — and the vocal stem is quiet (quiet_db under its own loud level) over at least quiet_share of the line’s coarse window, including one unbroken quiet stretch of min_quiet_s. The window is shifted by the vendor’s measured bias (median fine-minus-coarse start over the words among trusts), and a window running past the end of the stem is not judged at all.

Both, because neither instrument is enough alone. Measured on the song that motivated this (muvid#144: a Suno transcript listing a repeated タン タン タン at 36-38 s that the stem shows nobody sang), the stem alone puts a sung, staccato ストップ ストップ at 0.63 quiet against the ghost’s 0.75 — vendor windows run on into the silence after a word, so a quiet window is common — while squeeze alone also flags a sung, fast スター ダンス (0.04 quiet). Together they separate the ghost and nothing else.

What they do NOT separate unaided is one short sung line whose vendor window is a second late: the aligner (searching ±``MARGIN_S``) misses it and squeezes, and the window lands on the silence after it — measured on the same song, the last line (フン) shifted +1 s was dropped. The unbroken min_quiet_s is what refuses that case: a short window cannot hold a second of silence, a ghost line is silence end to end.

The unit is the line, because a transcript repeats lines, and because the first ghost word’s own window held the decaying tail of the previous held note: sound, but not its sound. among is squeeze and not disagreement on purpose — a line the vendor MISPLACED was sung somewhere else, and dropping it would delete a sung line. With the stem, lost() widens among only by disagreeing words that are off every onset AND sit in another line’s window, which a misplaced-but-sung line does not produce (see stem_detectors()).

Detector

muvid.lyricvid.glyph_align.with_spans(tt, *, vocals=None, max_span_s=0.6, search_s=(0.18, 0.08), silence_db=-40.0, valley_db=8.0, flat_db=1.0, frame_s=0.01)[source]¶

Give every sung glyph a span: where its sound runs (muvid#149).

A glyph’s end is nominal (20-140 ms after its onset), so a consumer that plays or wipes per glyph needs this. Each Span runs from the glyph’s onset to the next sung glyph’s onset, capped at max_span_s after the glyph’s start; its cut says how that trailing boundary was decided. ー extends the glyph before it (it is no boundary and has no span); ッ ends it (its sokuon is silence) and has no span; punctuation, a glyph with sung=False and one with no finite start have none either and are no boundary.

With vocals (the separated vocal stem; none is needed) each boundary is refined from the stem’s levels, searched from search_s[0] before the next onset to search_s[1] after it, never before the glyph’s own span start: silence (the previous span ends where the sound died and the next starts where it returns, or at its own onset if it does not return within the search), valley (cut at the flat bottom of a dip between the syllables) or legato (no clean cut: the onset, flagged as such). Before a ッ a silence or valley is used the same way, else next. Without a stem every boundary is next. Any end clamped at max_span_s is cap. A span may start later than start (after a silence); start is not moved. Spans never overlap, and times are rounded to the ms. A burst (a spectral-flux attack) is not detected: those boundaries read as legato.

Run it LAST, after reconcile(), apply_timing_overrides() and repair_spills(): it recomputes every span from scratch (running it twice changes nothing), and each of those passes clears EVERY span when it changes anything, since a span depends on its neighbours.

Return type:

TimedText
