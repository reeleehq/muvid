muvid.footage.sound

muvid.footage.sound¶

The clips’ own sound under the song — and the song kept removable.

A music video’s audio is the clean song and nothing else (the assembler mutes every clip). A promo, a recap or a class video is different: the picture is cut to the music, but the people in it talk, breathe and laugh, and that sound (“production sound”, “nat sound”) is part of what sells it. Editors keep it on its own track under a music bed and duck the music when someone speaks; and because the music is the part most often replaced (a licence, a client’s taste, a better track found later), they deliver it as stems — the music, the production sound, and the mix — rather than one baked track.

This module is that, for a rendered footage edit:

  • song_stem() is the music as the render plays it (cut to the edit’s window, faded at its tail when the window ends early), taken from the song itself, not decoded back out of the render (each AAC pass shifts audio by the encoder’s priming).

  • clip_sound_track() builds the production-sound stem from the same cuts the picture was rendered from: each video cut contributes its own audio for exactly its span (from its in-point, on the clip’s own clock: an audio stream that starts after its video is padded, not pulled early) at its own level (EdlEntry.sound_db), with a short fade at each edge so a cut does not click; a photo, a gap, a retimed cut (slow motion has no sync sound) or a clip with no audio stream is silence. Its length is the render’s, sample for sample.

  • the clips’ sound is split in two stems by each cut’s level: dialogue (the cuts meant to be heard: soundbites, nat pops) and nat (the room under every other shot). mix_under() raises the dialogue toward a speech level, ducks the music under it alone (mixing.audio.duck_audio, the fleet’s one ducker) by as much as puts the speech SPEECH_OVER_MUSIC_DB over the music, lays the nat sound in as a bed, and limits the sum to PEAK_DBFS.

  • mux_audio() puts one audio track on a picture, compensating the AAC encoder’s priming so the sound lands on the frame it was cut to.

The render op (muvid.footage.service.render(), sound="song_and_clips") is their one caller and records the stems and the mix parameters beside final.mp4. Research behind the numbers: misc/docs/research/general/promo_video_editing_and_music.md.

muvid.footage.sound.aac_priming_filter(*, priming_samples=1024, sample_rate=48000)[source]¶

The -af links that put an AAC encode’s sound back on its picture (muvid#379).

The encoder prepends priming_samples of priming, and a file with no edit list (the delivery contract) plays them, so every AAC pass lands the sound 21.3 ms late at 48 kHz; measured with a click at 1.000 s, decoded at 1.021 s after one pass and 1.043 s after two. Trimming as many samples off the INPUT cancels it, and padding them back at the tail keeps the length. aresample comes first so start_sample counts samples at the rate the encoder primes at, whatever rate the input has. Append it LAST to a chain whose timeline is the delivered one (a fade’s st is read before the trim). Every FOOTAGE AAC encode goes through it: mux_audio() and muvid.footage.assemble._audio_args(). The visualizer and the lyric-video renderers do not yet (muvid#385).

Return type:

str

muvid.footage.sound.clip_sound_track(cuts, out_path, *, edge_fade_s=0.04, default_db=0.0, sample_rate=48000, keep=None)[source]¶

Write the production-sound stem of cuts (AssemblyCut-like: song_start, song_end, clip_id, clip_in, clip_path, rate, sound_db) to out_path (wav).

The stem starts at cuts[0].song_start and is exactly as long as the cuts span: each sounding cut is its clip’s audio from clip_in for the cut’s duration at its sound_db (default_db when None), padded with silence where the clip’s audio starts late or runs out; every other cut is silence, and so is every cut keep(cut, level) rejects (how the dialogue and nat stems are split).

muvid.footage.sound.mix_under(music, dialogue, nat, out_path, *, clips_out=None, speech_target_dbfs=-20.0, speech_over_music_db=12.0, duck_db=-8.0, max_duck_db=-30.0, active_dbfs=-55.0, peak_dbfs=-1.0)[source]¶

Mix music over the clips’ sound, split as dialogue and nat (wav stems of equal length), into out_path:

  • the dialogue is raised toward speech_target_dbfs (never lowered), and the music ducked under it (mixing.audio.duck_audio, keyed on the dialogue alone) by as much as puts the speech speech_over_music_db over the music, between duck_db and max_duck_db;

  • the nat sound (the room under the other shots) is laid in as it is, never ducking anything;

  • all of it summed in floating point and scaled down (never up) so the peak stays at or under peak_dbfs.

clips_out (optional) receives the clips’ sound as mixed (dialogue at its gain + nat): with the ducked music it sums to the mix. Returns the parameters that made it (dialogue gain, duck depth, speech and music levels, the limiter’s gain).

Return type:
